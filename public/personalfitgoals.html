01 / Read before accepting

Your starting conditions

This is a provisional adult beginner sprint-distance plan, not a personalized prescription or an Ironman plan. It assumes you can walk briskly for 30 minutes, cycle safely for 20 minutes, and swim 25 m comfortably in a supervised pool. Training starts around 3 hours/week and peaks around 6 hours, plus travel and changing.

Cannot meet an entry condition? Spend 2–4+ weeks on walking, easy cycling and coached swim lessons first; shift the finish date. A non-swimmer should prioritize lessons, not attempt the distance sets. If you are currently ill, feverish or unusually breathless, postpone training. Seek medical advice before vigorous exercise if inactive or if you have a relevant health condition; chest pain, fainting or severe breathlessness means stop and seek urgent care.

02 / Campaign map

Build the athlete. Then test the system.

Weeks 01–04 · E rank arc

Awakening

Build routine, water confidence and run/walk tolerance. Everything easy. Week 4 reduces load.

Checkpoint: calm 4 × 50 m pool swim, 40-minute ride, 25-minute run/walk.
Weeks 05–08 · D rank arc

Foundation

Two sessions per sport each week; optional walking off the bike. Practice braking, gears and breathing.

Checkpoint: 200 m continuous pool swim, 60-minute ride, 30-minute run/walk.
Weeks 09–12 · C rank arc

Endurance

Longer easy sets. Introduce short bike-to-run bricks and controlled bike efforts only if recovering well.

Checkpoint: 400 m continuous, 70-minute ride, 35-minute easy run/walk.
Weeks 13–16 · B rank arc

Integration

Combine skills, test food and learn supervised open-water swimming when ready.

Checkpoint: 600 m pool swim; calm supervised open-water practice; 75-minute ride.
Weeks 17–20 · A rank arc

Race craft

Rehearse kit, transitions and even pacing. Keep recovery weeks easy.

Checkpoint: 750 m comfortable swim in relevant conditions; 20 km bike; 5 km run/walk separately.
Weeks 21–26 · S rank arc

Final gate

Controlled dress rehearsal, consolidation, then a two-week taper. Freshness beats last-minute fitness.

Checkpoint: equipment, swim safety, cutoffs, fueling and pacing all rehearsed.

Checkpoints are comfort checks, not all-out tests. They replace part of a scheduled session. If unmet, repeat the last manageable week and move the race goal. Displayed ranks are motivation, never evidence of fitness.

SIDE QUEST / 12-WEEK WINTER ARC

Forge strength. Keep your endurance.

12 October 2026 – 3 January 2027 by default: 84 days of strength, Indian home food and recovery. A three-month habit and performance challenge; visible body changes vary. This complements your triathlon campaign.

Arc consistency
Optional check-ins. Rest and adaptations count.
Selected checkpoint

Daily foundations

Eat regular meals. Include protein, vegetables and fruit. Keep a consistent sleep window. Follow the scheduled session or choose needed recovery.

One schedule. Strength A and B replace the existing Tuesday and Thursday strength sessions. Keep the campaign's time caps; do fewer exercises if needed. Do not add extra cardio, punishment workouts or a crash diet. In taper weeks, follow the triathlon taper instead.
Weeks 1–4 / Awaken

Build the foundation

Learn controlled movement. Start with one set of each exercise, 8–12 comfortable repetitions, leaving 3–4 repetitions in reserve. Week 4: halve the sets and review recovery.

Weeks 5–8 / Forge

Progress patiently

If time and recovery allow, use two sets. When all repetitions feel controlled over two sessions, increase repetitions, then use a small load increase. Week 8: halve sets.

Weeks 9–12 / Ascend

Consolidate your gains

Keep two sets within time caps. Avoid failure and max lifts. Week 12: halve sets and compare comfortable technique, consistency and energy with week 1.

Strength A / Tuesday

  • Chair squat or goblet squat · 8–12 reps.
  • Wall or incline push-up · 8–12 reps.
  • Band or supported dumbbell row · 8–12 reps.
  • Dead bug · 6–8 controlled reps per side.

Strength B / Thursday

  • Glute bridge or light hip hinge · 8–12 reps.
  • Low step-up with support · 6–10 per side.
  • Band or supported dumbbell row · 8–12 reps.
  • Side plank from knees · 15–25 seconds per side.

Warm up gently for 3–5 minutes within the session cap; rest 60–90 seconds as needed. Use the easier variation first. Sharp pain means stop and modify. On amber readiness, reduce or skip strength; red means rest. Get coaching for unfamiliar loaded movements.

Weekly quest board

At weeks 1, 4, 8 and 12, compare the same comfortable squat/push-up variation and your energy. No maximum tests, compulsory weigh-ins or before/after photos.

Indian fuel system

These are meal templates, not a calorie prescription. Start with familiar portions: rice/roti/millet, a substantial protein dish, vegetables and fruit through the day. Add carbohydrate around longer training and extra food if hungry or recovering poorly. Avoid cutting meals to compensate for food.

Protein & portions

A practical starter plate: 1–2 rotis or a serving of cooked rice, a generous serving of your protein dish, and vegetables. Adjust all portions to appetite, training and professional advice; this is not an upper limit. Include a fruit and a small handful of nuts through the day if tolerated.

Dal alone in a small bowl may not make a substantial protein meal. Combine pulses with tofu, dairy, eggs or another selected protein. Product labels and recipes differ.

Training snacks & swaps

Before training: a banana, toast or a small familiar idli portion if needed. After: your next balanced meal; if delayed, fruit plus curd/milk or fortified soy drink. Keep high-fibre beans and rich curries away from sessions if they upset your stomach.

Rice and roti both fit. Choose affordable seasonal foods; supplements and detox drinks are optional and unnecessary for this challenge. Use allergy-safe alternatives; fortified soy products can replace dairy. For restrictive diets or a body-composition goal, get individual guidance.

Weekly shopping & preparation

Stock rice/atta/millets, dal, rajma/chana, your selected protein foods, seasonal vegetables, fruit, nuts and cooking oil. Soak and cook beans thoroughly. Batch-cook safe portions; refrigerate promptly and reheat thoroughly. Prep two protein dishes and chopped vegetables so busy days stay simple.

Food variety follows ICMR–NIN guidance. The 12-week structure and meal combinations are this app's practical templates, not a clinical transformation protocol.

04 / Skill tree

How to execute each session

Effort: use a 0–10 scale

  • RPE 2–3: very easy recovery, relaxed breathing.
  • RPE 3–4: easy endurance, comfortable full sentences. This is most of the plan.
  • RPE 5–6: controlled steady effort; short phrases. Only the optional bike blocks use this.
  • No maximal efforts, mandatory pace targets or estimated heart-rate zones. Slow down for hills, heat and fatigue.

CDC: talk test and exercise intensity.

Run/walk is a race strategy

Weeks 1–4: start with 1 minute jogging / 2 minutes walking. Weeks 5–8: try 2/2, then 3/1 only if comfortable. Later: extend easy jogging gradually or retain walk breaks all the way to race day.

Spend the first and last 5 minutes walking or jogging very easily. Avoid increasing speed and duration together. Persistent pain, altered gait or next-day worsening: stop running and get assessed.

Swim A / technique and Swim B / endurance

Start with 5 minutes easy swimming or coached water skills. Use 5–10 minutes for relaxed exhalation underwater, body position and gentle side breathing under instruction. Finish with about 5 easy minutes. In the remaining time, use the week's repeat length; rest 20–40 seconds between repeats, more if needed. Stop at the time cap even below the distance ceiling.

Swim A: short clean repeats. Swim B: longer relaxed repeats. As able, replace repeats with a continuous checkpoint swim; do not add it on top. Avoid breath-holding challenges and hyperventilation. A pool coach is the highest-value early upgrade.

Bike A / control and Bike B / endurance

Begin and finish with 5–10 easy minutes. Bike A develops safe turning, braking, gear changes and a comfortable seated cadence on a traffic-free area or trainer. From week 9, a non-recovery Bike A may contain 3 × 3 minutes at RPE 5–6 with 3 easy minutes between, within the scheduled duration. Otherwise ride easy. Bike B stays conversational throughout.

Wear a correctly fitted helmet, inspect brakes/tires, carry identification and avoid headphones in traffic. Practice drinking only in a safe environment. A stationary bike counts for fitness; handling still needs separate safe practice.

Brick / bike directly into an easy run or walk

After Saturday's bike, change shoes, secure the bike and begin the listed short run/walk. The first 3–5 minutes should feel almost too easy. This teaches coordination; it is not a speed test. Before week 9, the listed five-minute brick is an optional walk.

Strength A & B / two short sessions

Strength A: chair squat or goblet squat, hip hinge, calf raise, row and dead bug. Strength B: low step-up or supported split squat, glute bridge, incline push-up, band row and side plank. Start with 1 set of 8–10 controlled reps each (core 15–25 seconds); progress to 2 sets of 8–12 within the time cap. Rest 45–90 seconds as needed. Leave 2–3 reps in reserve; no failure training.

Use light resistance and pain-free range. Recovery weeks: one easy set. Weeks 25–26: mobility only instead of strength. Add a few minutes of ankle, hip and thoracic mobility after sessions when useful.

Open water is a separate skill. From week 13, replace one pool session with a short supervised venue session only after you can swim 400 m calmly and the coach/venue approves. Never swim alone. Check water quality, weather and local conditions; use a bright cap and venue-approved tow float, and stay near an exit. Practice sighting, floating and signalling for help. Enter gradually; leave if cold, panicked or unwell. A float is not a lifeguard. Build duration under supervision; pool distance alone does not establish open-water readiness.
05 / Restore mana

Fuel the training. Protect the recovery.

Everyday meals

Eat enough to maintain energy and recovery; this is not a crash-diet plan. Include carbohydrate at main meals, a protein source at each meal, vegetables/fruit and dietary fats. Increase carbohydrate portions on longer days.

  • Before an early session: banana or toast if hungry; for a longer morning, a familiar light meal with time to digest.
  • Breakfast: idli + sambar + eggs/curd, or oats + milk/soy milk + fruit.
  • Lunch: rice/roti + dal + vegetables + paneer/tofu/chicken/fish.
  • After training: a regular carb-and-protein meal; milk/curd or soy drink plus fruit if the meal is delayed.
  • Dinner: rice/roti, protein and cooked vegetables. Avoid experimenting before long sessions.

Supplements are optional, not a prerequisite. Use a sports dietitian for individual energy/protein needs, deficiencies or restrictive diets.

Long-session fuel & fluids

For easy sessions under an hour, water and normal meals are usually enough. For exercise lasting 1–2.5 hours, practice 30–60 g carbohydrate/hour, starting at the lower end and adjusting for tolerance. Count carbohydrate from all drinks, food and gels together.

Example: one gel with 25 g carbohydrate plus 500 ml drink containing 30 g = 55 g; labels vary. Take gels with water as directed. Test on long rides, never for the first time at the race.

Drink to thirst and adapt to your sweat rate and weather; do not force liters or finish heavier from overdrinking. An electrolyte drink can help during hot, sweaty or longer sessions. No universal salt dose fits everyone.

AIS: sports drinks and carbohydrate guidance

Sleep & rest

Protect a consistent sleep schedule, generally 7–9 hours for adults. Monday is genuine rest. Persistent heavy legs, irritability or declining performance: reduce training and review food, sleep and stress.

Heat & conditions

Choose cooler hours, shade or indoor training in hot/humid weather. Reduce pace and duration. Build heat exposure gradually; check local air quality and storm conditions. Stop for dizziness, confusion or unusual weakness.

Daily readiness rule

Green: proceed. Amber: shorten by 25–50%, keep easy or rest. Red: skip training and address symptoms. No XP penalty for choosing recovery. Never “repay” missed sessions.

06 / Inventory

Gear that earns its place

Essentials first

  • Pool access or lessons; swimsuit, well-fitting goggles, cap.
  • Safe serviced bicycle, helmet, working brakes, bottle/cage, pump, spare tube/repair kit and tire levers.
  • Comfortable running shoes, socks, suitable sports clothing and sun protection.
  • Race entry, travel, identification and any organizer-required membership/medical documents.

Allocate money to swim instruction, bike service/fit and shoes before gadgets. Borrow a suitable bike where practical.

Optional or event-dependent

  • Tri suit, race belt, anti-chafe product and small transition towel.
  • Wetsuit only when appropriate for water temperature and permitted by the event; practice in it first.
  • GPS watch, smart trainer and clipless pedals are optional. Familiar flat pedals can simplify a first race.
  • Open-water safety equipment required by the supervised venue.

Check the actual event's bicycle, wetsuit, drafting and equipment rules before buying anything.

07 / Final gate

The finish line is a process.

Readiness checklist

If a safety or cutoff item remains unresolved, choose a later race, pool-based sprint, shorter event or relay. Completion of this dashboard does not guarantee race readiness.

Race execution

  1. 48 hours before: inspect bike, pack kit, check weather and briefing; eat familiar meals and sleep normally.
  2. Race morning: familiar breakfast 2–3 hours before if tolerated, normal sipping, arrive with setup time.
  3. Swim: start conservatively, choose space, settle breathing. Signal safety support if distressed.
  4. T1: goggles/cap off, shoes on; helmet fastened before touching/unracking bike. Walk/run to mount line.
  5. Bike: easy opening minutes, controlled effort, follow drafting rules and tested fueling.
  6. T2: dismount before line; rack bike before unfastening helmet. Change shoes and take race number as required.
  7. Run: first kilometer easy; use planned walk breaks. Build only if you feel strong.
  8. After: walk, drink to thirst, eat and recover. Seek race medical support for concerning symptoms.
Dress rehearsal, week 22: Saturday's scheduled brick becomes 20 km bike + 2 km easy run/walk if each fits inside the time cap; otherwise keep the timed session. Friday's swim is the separate swim rehearsal. Practice kit, transitions and food. No all-out full triathlon is required.
